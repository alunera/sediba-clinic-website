BUSINESS REQUIREMENT:

Sediba Wellness requires a 100% deposit/payment to secure an appointment.

The appointment must NOT be considered confirmed until the required payment has successfully been completed.

This requirement comes directly from the Sediba client discussion, where the owner specifically requested 100% deposit payment to prevent time-wasters.

GOAL:

Add a secure payment step to the existing booking flow.

PAYMENT REQUIREMENT:

The customer must pay 100% of the treatment price shown on the Services/Booking system.

Use the existing treatment pricing already implemented.

DO NOT create a second pricing system.

The payment amount must be retrieved from the same central treatment data already used by the booking system.

BOOKING STATUS:

Implement clear booking/payment states.

At minimum:

- Pending Payment
- Payment Successful / Paid
- Payment Failed
- Cancelled

A booking should only become CONFIRMED after successful payment.

PAYMENT FLOW:

1. Customer selects treatment.
2. Customer selects available date.
3. Customer selects available time.
4. Customer enters required customer details.
5. Customer sees a final booking summary:

Treatment:
[Name]

Price:
[R amount]

Date:
[Date]

Time:
[Time]

6. Customer proceeds to payment.
7. Customer completes payment.
8. Backend verifies successful payment.
9. Booking is marked as Paid/Confirmed.
10. Customer receives confirmation.

IMPORTANT:

Do not mark a booking as confirmed simply because the customer reached the payment page.

The backend must verify the payment result.

AVAILABILITY / SLOT PROTECTION:

The payment system must work correctly with the existing availability system.

We must avoid double booking.

Consider the following scenario:

Customer A selects:

20 August
10:30

Customer A proceeds to payment.

Another customer must not be able to successfully book the same slot at the same time.

Implement appropriate temporary reservation/locking behaviour if required by the payment provider.

If payment fails or the customer abandons payment, the temporary reservation should eventually be released according to a sensible timeout.

Do not permanently block appointment slots because someone simply opened the payment page.
